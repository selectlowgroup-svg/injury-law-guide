Bellevue Car Accident Guide 2026: What to Do, Washington Laws and Deadlines

By Parham Hakimi, Bellevue Accident Lawyers – Bellevue, WA. General information, not legal advice.

Bellevue car accident lawyer quick answer: After a car accident in Bellevue, get medical care, call the police, take photos and swap insurance details. In Washington you usually have 3 years to file an injury lawsuit (RCW 4.16.080), and you can recover money even if you were partly at fault (RCW 4.22.005).

How common are serious crashes in Washington?

The Washington Traffic Safety Commission counted 731 traffic deaths and 3,290 serious injuries in Washington in 2024 (early numbers). Impaired drivers were involved in 348 of those deaths, and speeding in 247.

King County, where Bellevue is, has the most traffic deaths of any county in the state. In 2023, 167 people died on King County roads.

In and around Bellevue, crash-prone roads include I-405, I-90, SR 520, Bellevue Way and NE 8th Street, where heavy commuter traffic and stop-and-go jams are common.

What to do right after a car accident in Bellevue

  1. Check for injuries and call 911.
  2. Call the police and get the report number.
  3. Take photos of the cars, the road, signs, the weather and your injuries.
  4. Swap details: name, phone, license, plate and insurance.
  5. Get witness names and numbers.
  6. See a doctor within a day or two, even if you feel fine.
  7. File a collision report if needed. If the police did not make a report, Washington may require you to file one yourself within a few days when someone was hurt or the damage is over a set amount.
  8. Tell your own insurance company and ask about PIP.
  9. Don't give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer before getting advice.
  10. Keep all bills, receipts and notes about missed work and pain.

How long do I have to file a car accident claim in Washington?

Usually 3 years from the date of the crash (RCW 4.16.080). Wrongful death claims also usually have 3 years.

Claims against a city, county or the state have extra steps. You must usually file a written claim first and wait a set time before you can sue.

Don't wait until the deadline. Evidence and witness memories fade fast.

Is Washington a no-fault state?

No. Washington is an at-fault state. The driver who caused the crash, and their insurer, pays for the harm. Insurers must offer PIP, which can pay some medical bills no matter who was at fault, unless you turned it down in writing.

Can I still get money if I was partly at fault in Washington?

Yes. Washington uses pure comparative fault (RCW 4.22.005). Your money is cut by your share of blame, but it is never wiped out.

Simple example: Your losses are $60,000 and you were 30% at fault. You could recover $42,000.

Insurers often try to blame you more to pay less, so good evidence matters.

How much car insurance must Washington drivers carry?

The minimum in Washington is:

  • $25,000 for injury to one person
  • $50,000 per crash for injuries
  • $10,000 for property damage

These amounts are low. Uninsured / underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage and PIP on your own policy can make a big difference.

What can I recover after a car accident in Washington?

Depending on the facts, a claim may include:

  • Medical bills, now and in the future
  • Lost pay
  • Car repair or replacement
  • Pain and suffering

Every case is different. No one can promise a result or an amount.

When should I talk to a Bellevue car accident lawyer?

  • You were hurt or missed work
  • The insurer is blaming you or offering too little
  • The other driver has little or no insurance
  • A truck, a rideshare car (Uber or Lyft), a business vehicle or a government vehicle was involved
  • Your own insurer is denying a fair claim (insurance bad faith)

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to sue after a car accident in Washington?

Usually 3 years from the crash (RCW 4.16.080).

Is Washington a no-fault state?

No. Washington is an at-fault state.

What if I was partly at fault?

You can still recover. Your amount goes down by your share (pure comparative fault, RCW 4.22.005).

What is PIP in Washington?

PIP is coverage on your own policy that pays some medical bills and lost pay no matter who caused the crash, unless you turned it down in writing.

Should I talk to the other driver's insurance company?

Give basic facts only. It's wise not to give a recorded statement before getting advice.
