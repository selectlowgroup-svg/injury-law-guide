What to Do After a Car Accident in Portland: 8 Steps

What to do after a car accident in Portland: 8 steps, Oregon&#x27;s 72-hour DMV rule, how PIP pays, and when to get checked.

What to do after a car accident in Portland: 8 steps, Oregon's 72-hour DMV rule, how PIP pays, and when to get checked.

After a car accident in Portland, do these things in this order: get safe, call 911 if anyone is hurt, trade information with the other driver, take photos, tell your insurance company, and get checked by a provider as soon as possible, ideally within 72 hours, even if you feel fine. Separately, Oregon law requires a DMV report in some crashes (step 2). I’m Parham Hakimi, Esq., Managing Attorney at Select Law. Here are the 8 steps.

What do I legally have to do after a car accident in Oregon?

I’m an attorney. This explains the rules. It is not legal advice.

  • Stop at the scene and stay. Give the other driver your name, address, insurance information and plate number. Help anyone who is hurt.
  • File a DMV crash report within 72 hours if anyone was hurt or killed, your car has more than $2,500 in damage, any car has more than $2,500 in damage and a car was towed, or other property has more than $2,500 in damage. Do this even if the police wrote a report.
  • If the crash has to be reported to the DMV, also tell the police right away, by 911 or the non-emergency line.
  • Keep your auto insurance in force and tell your insurer about the crash. Most policies require prompt notice.
  • Oregon gives you a limited time to sue for an injury. The general limit is two years, with exceptions. An attorney can tell you how it applies to you.

Step 1: Is anyone hurt? Check for injuries and get safe

Check yourself and your passengers. Call 9-1-1 if anyone is unconscious, bleeding, in severe pain, confused, or having trouble breathing.

If it is safe, move the cars out of traffic and turn on your hazard lights. Stay nearby until help arrives.

If everyone seems fine, still plan to get checked. Whiplash, headaches and back pain often show up hours or days later. I cover this in pain that shows up days after a crash and whiplash treatment in Portland.

Step 2: Who do I call and what do I report?

Call 911 if anyone is hurt, a driver seems impaired, or the cars block traffic. For other crashes, call the Portland non-emergency line at 503-823-3333.

Oregon also requires you to file your own crash report with the DMV within 72 hours if anyone was hurt or killed, your car has more than $2,500 in damage, any car has more than $2,500 in damage and a car was towed, or other property has more than $2,500 in damage. Do this even if police came and wrote a report. If you are not sure, file it.

The form is the Oregon Traffic Collision and Insurance Report (Form 735-32). You can file it online with the Oregon DMV. Skipping it can cost you your license, even if the crash was not your fault.

Step 3: What should I write down and photograph?

Take photos of the other driver’s:

  • Driver’s license
  • Insurance card
  • License plate

Then photograph the scene: both vehicles, the road, street signs, and any visible injuries. Ask witnesses for a name and phone number. Do not guess about fault at the scene. Stick to the facts.

Step 4: When do I tell my insurance company?

Tell your own insurer right away, even if the other driver was at fault. Most policies require prompt notice.

Oregon auto policies include Personal Injury Protection (PIP). PIP pays reasonable and necessary medical bills after a crash, no matter who caused it. The minimum is $15,000 in medical benefits. Care generally has to happen within two years of the crash. PIP can cover chiropractic care, massage and rehab.

Oregon is an at-fault state. PIP is the exception: it pays no matter who was at fault. Injury costs beyond PIP still depend on who caused the crash.

If the other driver has no insurance, your own uninsured motorist coverage may apply. Oregon policies must carry it. You can lower it, but not reject it. Underinsured coverage pays only for costs above the at-fault driver’s policy limits. I explain this in UM/UIM after a crash. The full rules are in the Oregon PIP insurance guide and on the Oregon Division of Financial Regulation site. For deadlines, see how long you have to file a PIP claim.

Step 5: Do I need a medical check if I feel fine?

Yes. Adrenaline can hide pain at first, and injured soft tissue can take 24 to 72 hours to start hurting. An early exam also starts your records.

ER, urgent care, or a chiropractor?

  • ER or 911 if you hit your head, passed out, have vision changes, severe neck or back pain, chest pain, numbness or weakness in an arm or leg, belly pain, trouble breathing, a seizure, a headache that keeps getting worse, or signs of a brain injury such as confusion, vomiting, slurred speech, or being very drowsy or hard to wake.
  • Urgent care within 24 hours if you have a clear minor injury, like a cut that needs stitches or a possibly broken finger.
  • A chiropractor as soon as possible, ideally within 72 hours, if you walked away fine but feel stiffness, mild headache, or neck or back tightness. This is especially true after a rear-end, T-bone or side-impact crash.

At the first medical visit after a crash, a provider will examine you and ask about the crash, screen for red flags, and refer you to imaging or a medical doctor if something looks serious. Not every crash needs a scan — see imaging after a car accident for when one does. Many providers can see you the same day or the next day.

For neck pain, see neck pain after a car accident. For timing, see how soon to see a chiropractor.

Care after a crash can include chiropractic adjustments, massage, and rehab exercises. A provider may also refer you to an acupuncturist or to a medical provider when you need one. Chiropractic care and rehab can help neck and back pain after a crash, but results vary and no outcome can be promised.

Step 6: How do I track my recovery?

Keep a simple log for the first two weeks. Write down where it hurts, your pain from 0 to 10, what helps or makes it worse, and how you slept. Keep every bill and appointment record.

PIP generally covers care for up to two years after the crash, or until your PIP limit (at least $15,000) is used. PIP is usually billed first. If PIP runs out, or you have no PIP, care can continue on a lien, where the bill is paid later from an injury settlement. See chiropractic care on a lien in Oregon.

Step 7: When should I talk to an attorney?

Consider one if you have a significant injury, the other driver was uninsured, or fault is disputed.

Keep notes from each visit. You can share them with your insurer or an attorney.

Step 8: How do I take care of my body and mind?

Rest, drink water, and keep moving gently. Stress after a crash is normal. Poor sleep and a short temper are common. If low mood or fear of driving lasts more than a few weeks, tell your doctor. A recovery plan should fit your symptoms, work and daily life.

Common questions

What should I do right after a car accident in Portland?

Check for injuries and move to a safe place if you can. Call 911 if anyone is hurt. Trade information with the other driver and take photos. Then tell your insurance company and get checked as soon as possible, ideally within 72 hours.

What do I legally have to do after a car accident in Oregon?

Stop, give your information, and help anyone who is hurt. Tell the police right away and file a DMV report within 72 hours if anyone was hurt or killed, your car has more than $2,500 in damage, any car has more than $2,500 in damage and a car was towed, or other property has more than $2,500 in damage. Do I need to see a doctor or chiropractor if I feel fine?

Yes. Adrenaline can hide pain at first, and injured soft tissue can take 24 to 72 hours to start hurting. Whiplash and soft-tissue injuries often show up later as stiffness or headaches. An early exam also starts your records.

How does Oregon PIP help after a car accident?

PIP pays reasonable and necessary medical bills after a crash, no matter who caused it. It can cover care like chiropractic, massage and rehab. The minimum is $15,000, and care generally has to happen within two years.

What if the other driver has no insurance?

Your own uninsured motorist (UM) coverage may apply. Oregon policies must include it. Underinsured (UIM) coverage pays only for costs above the at-fault driver’s policy limits.
