Lake Oswego Car Accident Guide 2026: What to Do, Oregon Laws and Deadlines

By Parham Hakimi, Select Law – Lake Oswego, OR. General information, not legal advice.

Lake Oswego car accident lawyer quick answer: After a car accident in Lake Oswego, get medical care, call the police, take photos and swap insurance details. In Oregon you must usually file a crash report with the DMV within 72 hours if anyone was hurt. You generally have 2 years to file an injury lawsuit (ORS 12.110), and you can recover only if you were not more than 50% at fault (ORS 31.600).

How common are car crashes in Oregon?

Oregon's Department of Transportation recorded 46,762 reportable crashes on all roads in 2023. Oregon DMV handles about 200,000 crash and insurance reports each year.

Around Lake Oswego, drivers use busy roads like I-5, I-205, Highway 43 (State Street), Boones Ferry Road and Country Club Road. Rain, dark winter evenings and rush-hour traffic all raise the risk.

What to do right after a car accident in Lake Oswego

  1. Check for injuries and call 911.
  2. Call the police and get the report number.
  3. Take photos of the cars, the road, signs, the weather and your injuries.
  4. Swap details with the other driver: name, phone, license, plate and insurance.
  5. Get witness names and numbers.
  6. See a doctor within a day or two, even if you feel okay.
  7. File your Oregon DMV crash report within 72 hours if required (see below).
  8. Tell your own insurance company and ask about your PIP benefits.
  9. Don't give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer before getting advice.
  10. Keep all bills, receipts and notes about missed work and pain.

Do I have to report a car accident to the Oregon DMV?

Yes, in many cases. You must send an Oregon Traffic Collision and Insurance Report to the DMV within 72 hours if any of these is true:

  • Someone was hurt or killed
  • Damage to your vehicle is over $2,500
  • Damage to any vehicle is over $2,500 and a vehicle was towed
  • Damage to other property is over $2,500

You still have to file this even if the police made their own report. If you don't file when required, the DMV must suspend your license. Keep a copy, because the DMV can't give you one later.

How long do I have to file a car accident claim in Oregon?

Usually 2 years from the crash for an injury lawsuit (ORS 12.110). Wrongful death claims usually have 3 years (ORS 30.020).

Claims against a city, county or the state have a much shorter notice rule, often 180 days under the Oregon Tort Claims Act.

What is PIP in Oregon?

PIP (Personal Injury Protection) is part of your own car insurance. It pays some of your medical bills and lost pay no matter who caused the crash. Every Oregon driver must carry at least $15,000 in PIP per person.

PIP helps fast, but it often doesn't cover everything. You can still make a claim against the driver who caused the crash.

Can I still get money if I was partly at fault in Oregon?

Yes, if your share of the fault is 50% or less. Oregon uses modified comparative fault (ORS 31.600). Your money is cut by your share of the blame. If you are found more than 50% at fault, you can't recover from the other driver.

Simple example: Your losses are $40,000 and you were 10% at fault. You could recover $36,000. If you were 60% at fault, you would recover nothing from the other driver.

That is why the other insurer may try hard to push more blame onto you.

How much car insurance must Oregon drivers carry?

The minimum in Oregon is:

  • $25,000 for injury to one person
  • $50,000 per crash for injuries
  • $20,000 for property damage
  • $15,000 in PIP per person
  • Uninsured motorist coverage is also required

Serious injuries can cost far more than these amounts, so check your own policy.

What can I recover after a car accident in Oregon?

Depending on the facts, a claim may include:

  • Medical bills, now and in the future
  • Lost pay
  • Car repair or replacement
  • Pain and suffering

Every case is different. No one can promise a result or an amount.

When should I talk to a Lake Oswego car accident lawyer?

  • You were hurt or missed work
  • The insurer says you were mostly at fault
  • Your PIP has run out
  • The other driver has little or no insurance
  • A truck, a business vehicle or a government vehicle was involved

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to sue after a car accident in Oregon?

Usually 2 years from the crash (ORS 12.110).

Do I have to report my accident to the Oregon DMV?

Yes, within 72 hours if anyone was hurt or damage is over $2,500, even if the police made a report.

Is Oregon a no-fault state?

No. Oregon is an at-fault state, but every driver must carry PIP, which pays some costs no matter who was at fault.

What if I was partly at fault?

You can recover if you were 50% or less at fault. Your amount goes down by your share (ORS 31.600).

Should I talk to the other driver's insurance company?

Give basic facts only. It's wise not to give a recorded statement before getting advice.
