Personal Injury Claims in Washington, Oregon and Arizona: 2026 Research Guide

By Parham Hakimi, personal injury attorney – Bellevue Accident Lawyers (Bellevue, WA) and Select Law (Lake Oswego, OR · Scottsdale, AZ). General information, not legal advice.

Personal injury lawyer quick answer: If someone else's carelessness hurt you, you may be able to make a personal injury claim for your medical bills, lost pay and pain. The deadline to file a lawsuit is usually 2 years in Arizona and Oregon and 3 years in Washington. How fault is shared, and some rules for dog bites, products and elder care, differ from state to state. This guide puts the rules side by side in plain words.

What is a personal injury claim?

A personal injury claim asks the person or company that caused your injury, usually through their insurance company, to pay for your losses. Most claims settle with the insurer. If they don't, the claim can become a personal injury lawsuit in court.

A claim may include:

  • Medical bills, now and in the future
  • Lost pay and lost ability to earn
  • Pain, suffering and loss of enjoyment of life
  • Property damage, like your car
  • Scarring, disfigurement and permanent limits

Car accident claims

Quick answer: All three states are at-fault states, so the driver who caused the crash pays. Get medical care, a police report, photos and the other driver's insurance details.

  • Arizona: 121,107 crashes and 1,228 deaths in 2024 (ADOT). 2-year deadline.
  • Oregon: 46,762 reportable crashes in 2023 (ODOT). You must file a DMV crash report within 72 hours if anyone was hurt or damage is over $2,500.
  • Washington: 731 traffic deaths and 3,290 serious injuries in 2024 (WTSC, early numbers). 3-year deadline.

Semi-truck and trucking accident claims

Quick answer: Truck crashes often involve more than one party who may be responsible: the driver, the trucking company, the cargo loader or a parts maker. Federal trucking rules on driver hours and truck upkeep can also matter.

Act fast. Trucking companies often send investigators to the scene within hours, and key records like driver logs and black-box data can be lost if no one asks for them early.

Motorcycle accident claims

Quick answer: Riders have the same rights as other drivers. Helmet laws differ:

  • Washington and Oregon: helmets required for all riders.
  • Arizona: helmets required for riders under 18.

Not wearing a helmet doesn't stop a claim, but the insurer may argue it made the head injury worse. In Oregon, a high share of fault can block recovery completely (the 51% rule).

Bicycle and pedestrian accident claims

Quick answer: Walkers and cyclists are hurt badly even at low speeds. In King County, Washington (where Bellevue is), 52 walkers and rollers were killed in 2024, according to the Washington Traffic Safety Commission.

The driver's insurance usually pays. Your own car policy's PIP or UIM coverage may also help, even if you were on foot or on a bike.

Dog bite and animal attack claims

Quick answer:

  • Washington (RCW 16.08.040) and Arizona (A.R.S. 11-1025): the owner is responsible for a bite in a public place, or if you were lawfully on private property, even if the dog never bit before.
  • Arizona note: a claim under the strict dog-bite statute may have only a 1-year deadline. Act quickly.
  • Oregon: no general strict-liability dog-bite statute. A claim usually needs proof the owner was careless or knew the dog was dangerous.

The dog owner's homeowner's or renter's insurance often pays these claims.

Slip and fall / premises liability claims

Quick answer: A store, landlord or property owner may have to pay if they knew, or should have known, about a danger like a wet floor, broken step or poor lighting and didn't fix it or warn you.

Take photos right away, report the fall to the manager, and get a copy of the incident report. The usual injury deadlines apply (2 years in AZ and OR, 3 years in WA).

Workplace injury claims

Quick answer: In all three states, workers' compensation is usually the main way to get paid for a work injury, and you generally can't sue your employer. But if someone other than your employer caused it (a driver, a contractor, or a maker of faulty equipment), you may also have a personal injury claim against them.

Workers' comp has its own short deadlines for reporting and filing, so report the injury to your employer right away.

Wrongful death claims

Quick answer: When a careless act causes a death, certain family members (or the estate) can file a wrongful death claim. Deadlines: 2 years in Arizona, 3 years in Oregon, 3 years in Washington. Who may file differs by state.

Brain, head, spinal and neck injury claims

Quick answer: These injuries can change a life and are often missed at first. Concussions, whiplash and disc injuries can show up hours or days later. See a doctor right away and follow every treatment plan, because gaps in care are something insurers often use to lower offers.

Catastrophic, permanent and scarring injuries

Quick answer: For life-changing injuries, a claim must count future costs: years of care, home changes, lost earning power and the daily impact of scars or disfigurement. These claims often need doctors, life-care planners and financial experts.

Washington and Arizona do not cap pain-and-suffering damages in ordinary injury cases. Oregon caps some non-economic damages in certain kinds of cases.

Defective and dangerous product claims

Quick answer: If a product was badly designed, badly made, or sold without proper warnings and it hurt you, the maker or seller may be responsible.

  • Washington: 3 years from when you found the harm and its cause (RCW 7.72.060). After 12 years, the product's "useful safe life" is usually presumed to be over.
  • Oregon: 2 years (ORS 30.905), plus an outer time limit counted from when the product was first bought.
  • Arizona: 2 years.

Keep the product, the box and the receipt. Don't throw anything away.

Nursing home abuse and neglect claims

Quick answer: Warning signs include bed sores, falls, sudden weight loss, unexplained bruises, poor hygiene and fear of staff. Each state has a special law protecting vulnerable adults:

  • Washington: Abuse of Vulnerable Adults Act (RCW 74.34)
  • Oregon: elder abuse civil action (ORS 124.100), which can allow triple damages
  • Arizona: Adult Protective Services Act (A.R.S. 46-455)

If someone is in danger, call 911 first. Then report it to the state's adult protective services.

How much does a personal injury lawyer cost?

Most personal injury lawyers work on a contingency fee. You pay nothing up front, and the lawyer is paid a share of the money recovered. If there is no recovery, there is usually no attorney fee. Ask for the fee terms in writing at your free consultation.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to file a personal injury claim?

Usually 3 years in Washington, and 2 years in Oregon and Arizona. Some claims (government, dog bite in Arizona) have shorter deadlines.

Can I recover if I was partly at fault?

Yes in Washington and Arizona, with your amount reduced by your share. In Oregon, only if you were 50% or less at fault.

Do I need a lawyer for a personal injury claim?

Not always. For minor claims with no injury, many people handle it themselves. If you were hurt, missed work or the insurer is blaming you, a free consultation can help.

How long does a personal injury claim take?

Simple claims can settle in a few months. Serious injuries often take longer, because it's wise to wait until doctors know the full picture.

What should I not say to the insurance company?

Don't guess, don't say you're fine, and don't give a recorded statement to the other side's insurer before getting advice.
