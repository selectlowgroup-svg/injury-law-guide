Scottsdale Car Accident Guide 2026: What to Do, Arizona Laws and Deadlines

By Parham Hakimi, Select Law – Scottsdale, AZ. General information, not legal advice.

Scottsdale car accident lawyer quick answer: After a car accident in Scottsdale, get medical care, call the police, take photos, and get the other driver's insurance details. In Arizona you usually have 2 years from the crash to file an injury lawsuit (A.R.S. 12-542), and you can still recover money even if you were partly at fault.

How common are car crashes in Arizona?

Very common. Arizona had 121,107 crashes and 1,228 traffic deaths in 2024, according to the Arizona Department of Transportation's yearly Crash Facts report. Speeding played a part in crashes that caused 417 deaths and 20,722 injuries that year. Alcohol was linked to about 28% of all traffic deaths.

In and around Scottsdale, busy roads like the Loop 101 (Pima Freeway), Shea Boulevard, Scottsdale Road and Hayden Road see heavy traffic every day, especially at rush hour and on weekend nights.

What to do right after a car accident in Scottsdale

Follow these steps if you can do them safely:

  1. Check for injuries and call 911. Your health comes first.
  2. Call the police. Ask for the report number. The police report is often the first thing an insurance company looks at.
  3. Take photos. Get the cars, the road, traffic signs, skid marks, the weather and your injuries.
  4. Swap details. Get the other driver's name, phone, license, plate number and insurance company.
  5. Talk to witnesses. Get their names and phone numbers.
  6. See a doctor within a day or two, even if you feel fine. Some injuries, like whiplash or a concussion, show up later.
  7. Tell your own insurance company about the crash, but keep it to the facts.
  8. Don't give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurance company before you get advice.
  9. Keep every bill and receipt, plus notes on missed work and daily pain.

How long do I have to file a car accident claim in Arizona?

Usually 2 years from the date of the crash. This rule is in Arizona Revised Statutes A.R.S. 12-542. If you miss it, a court will most likely throw out your case.

There are some important exceptions:

  • Claims against a city, county or the state: You must file a written notice of claim within 180 days and file the lawsuit within 1 year (A.R.S. 12-821 and 12-821.01). This matters if a city vehicle or a badly kept road caused your crash.
  • Children: For someone under 18, the 2-year clock usually doesn't start until they turn 18.

The insurance claim itself should be reported much sooner. Most policies require quick notice.

Is Arizona a no-fault state?

No. Arizona is an at-fault state. The driver who caused the crash, and their insurance company, pays for the harm. You can make a claim with their insurer, with your own insurer, or file a lawsuit.

Can I still get money if I was partly at fault?

Yes. Arizona uses pure comparative fault (A.R.S. 12-2505). Your money is cut by your share of the blame, but it is never wiped out.

Simple example: Your total losses are $50,000, and you were 20% at fault. You could recover $40,000.

Insurance companies often try to push more of the blame onto you, because every percent lowers what they pay.

How much car insurance must Arizona drivers carry?

The legal minimum in Arizona is:

  • $25,000 for injury to one person
  • $50,000 for injuries to two or more people
  • $15,000 for property damage

These amounts are low compared with the cost of a hospital stay. That is why your own uninsured / underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage can matter a lot. Check your policy.

What can I recover after a car accident in Arizona?

Depending on the facts, a claim may include:

  • Medical bills, now and in the future
  • Lost pay and lost ability to work
  • Car repair or replacement
  • Pain, suffering and loss of enjoyment of life

Every case is different. No one can promise a result or an amount.

When should I talk to a Scottsdale car accident lawyer?

It helps to talk to a lawyer early if:

  • You were hurt, or you missed work
  • The other driver's insurer is blaming you
  • The other driver has no insurance or too little insurance
  • A truck, a business vehicle or a government vehicle was involved
  • You are close to a deadline

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to sue after a car accident in Arizona?

Usually 2 years from the crash date (A.R.S. 12-542). Claims against a public body have a 180-day notice rule and a 1-year limit.

Is Arizona a no-fault car insurance state?

No. Arizona is an at-fault state, so the driver who caused the crash is responsible.

What if I was partly to blame?

You can still recover. Your amount goes down by your share of fault (pure comparative fault, A.R.S. 12-2505).

Should I talk to the other driver's insurance company?

You can give basic facts, but it's wise not to give a recorded statement or sign anything before getting advice.

Do I need a lawyer for a minor accident?

Not always. If you were not hurt and the damage is small, you may handle it yourself. If you were injured, a free consultation can help you understand your options.
